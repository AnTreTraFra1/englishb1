About the Ancient City

Elea, later called Velia, is an ancient Greek city on the coast of Campania, in southern Italy. It was founded in 540 BC by Greek settlers from Phocaea.

🏛️

Ancient History

Founded by Phocaean Greeks in 540 BC after the Battle of Alalia. The city grew into a rich and powerful centre for trade in the Mediterranean.

A City Between Two Names

Elea was the Greek name of the city. When the Romans took control in 88 BC, they changed the name to Velia. But the city kept its Greek identity. People continued to speak Greek and write in Greek for many centuries.

The city is located on a promontory between two rivers, the Palistro and the Fiumarella. In ancient times, it had two harbours and a strong defensive wall. Today, the coastline has changed, and the ancient harbours are now inland.

Velia is part of the Cilento, Vallo di Diano and Alburni National Park, a UNESCO World Heritage site.

The Eleatic School

Parmenides founded the Eleatic School in the 5th century BC. He was one of the most important philosophers of ancient Greece. His student Zeno created famous paradoxes, like the story of Achilles and the tortoise.

The school taught that reality is one and unchanging. This idea was very different from other Greek philosophers, who believed that everything changes.
