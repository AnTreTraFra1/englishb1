Explore the Archaeological Park

Walk through the remains of a Greek and Roman city. Discover Porta Rosa, the Acropolis, the theatre, the baths, and much more.

Main Monuments

Here are the most important monuments you can see at the archaeological park of Velia.

🏛️

Porta Rosa

Porta Rosa is the most famous monument in Velia. It is not a gate, but a viaduct with a semicircular arch. It was built in the 4th century BC and is one of the oldest arch bridges in the world. It connects the northern and southern parts of the city. It was discovered in 1964 by the archaeologist Mario Napoli, who named it after his wife Rosa.

🏔️

The Acropolis

The Acropolis is the highest part of the city. It has been inhabited since the foundation of Elea in 540 BC. On the upper terrace, you can see the remains of a large temple dedicated to Athena, a medieval tower, and the Palatine Chapel. From the top, you have a beautiful view of the coast and the surrounding hills.

🎭

The Theatre

The theatre could hold about 2,000 spectators. It was built in the Greek period and later changed by the Romans. The seating area has 20 to 22 rows of stone seats, divided into five sections. The theatre is built into the hillside and offers a panoramic view of the landscape.

🛁

The Baths

There are two bath complexes in Velia. The oldest one (Hellenistic period, 3rd century BC) was a public bathhouse open to all citizens. The Roman baths are larger and more luxurious, with marble decoration, painted plaster, and a complex heating system. Both buildings show how important public bathing was in ancient times.

🏘️

The Agora and the Quarters

The agora was the public square, the heart of social and economic life. Around it, you can see the remains of houses, shops, and public buildings. The Archaic quarter is the oldest part of the city, with houses built in a regular grid of streets. The southern quarter contains a large domus (Roman house) with a central courtyard and over thirty rooms.

🪖

Recent Discoveries (2022–2026)

In 2022, archaeologists discovered an archaic temple of Athena on the Acropolis, dating to around 540 BC. Inside, they found three bronze helmets — two Greek and one Etruscan. These helmets are war trophies from the Battle of Alalia. This is the first physical evidence of the battle described by the historian Herodotus. The exhibition "Exiles and Victors: The Helmets of Velia" opened in May 2026.

The Exhibition: "Exiles and Victors"

In May 2026, the Italian Ministry of Culture opened a major exhibition called "Exiles and Victors: The Helmets of Velia". The exhibition presents the three bronze helmets together with hundreds of other objects found in the temple of Athena.

The exhibition tells the story of the Phocaean people as refugees who built a new home after a great battle. It shows how a military disaster became the foundation of one of the most important cultural and philosophical centres of the ancient world.
